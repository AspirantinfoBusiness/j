# Kashmir Observer × Healthcare: Presentation Script

**Length:** about 9 minutes at a calm pace (roughly 1,100 words), plus Q&A.
**Audience:** the Editor / Director of Kashmir Observer.
**Tip:** don't read word for word. Learn the **bold opening line** of each slide and speak the rest naturally. Every slide's script is also in the PowerPoint speaker notes (View → Notes, or Presenter View).

---

## Slide 1: Title · 0:00 – 0:30

**Do:** stand, make eye contact, pause before speaking.

**Good morning.** Thank you for this opportunity. Over the last few weeks I studied our media kit, the 2026 rate card and the PRGI circulation certificate, and I asked one question: which industry should Kashmir Observer go after next, and how do we win it? My answer is healthcare. In the next nine minutes I'll show you why, who we should approach, what we should sell, and how we can start within ninety days.

## Slide 2: The opportunity on one page · 0:30 – 1:10

**Here is the whole idea on one page.** First, the market: healthcare is a high-trust, high-value category, and in J&K it is growing fast. Second, the media: Kashmir Observer offers exactly what health brands need, which is credibility and reach across print, web, video and social. Third, the money: I've built three packages straight from our rate card, from sixty-eight thousand rupees to about seven-point-eight lakh, and a plan that could bring roughly twenty-four lakh in a first cycle.

## Slide 3: Why healthcare · 1:10 – 2:05

**Before choosing healthcare, I compared five sectors.** I scored each on five points: how much the buyer depends on trust, ticket size, fit with our products, white space, and growth. These scores are my own judgment, not market data. But the reasoning is clear. Banking and automobiles are strong, but HDFC, J&K Bank, Bajaj Allianz, Maruti and Nissan already advertise with us, so the new money there is limited. Education is crowded; coaching ads are already on our front page. Healthcare is the one category where trust matters most and where we still have open space. And as the brief asked, I left out hospitality, fashion and cold storage.

## Slide 4: Our audience · 2:05 – 2:50

**Our biggest selling point is that our numbers are verified.** The Press Registrar General has certified eighty thousand two hundred and fifty sold copies per day. In a market with more than three hundred newspapers, an audited number is rare. Online, we have about two million page views a month, sixty-eight point nine million Facebook views last year, two hundred and five thousand Facebook followers, and eighty-one thousand YouTube subscribers. So one advertiser can reach readers in print, on the web, on video and on social, at home and in the diaspora.

## Slide 5: The market · 2:50 – 3:40

**Now the market itself.** J&K's health budget for 2025-26 is about eight thousand eight hundred crore rupees, up four hundred and eighty crore. Medical colleges went from four in 2019 to seventeen in 2025. There are over twenty-two thousand hospital beds, about eighty-three lakh SEHAT golden cards, and two hundred and fifty-one hospitals empanelled under PM-JAY SEHAT, a hundred and thirty-three of them private. Because of insurance, patients can now choose their hospital. When patients can choose, hospitals need to advertise. That is our opening.

## Slide 6: Who we reach · 3:40 – 4:20

**A health advertiser wants four kinds of people, and we reach all four.** Patients and families read our daily paper. The diaspora and visitors read KO Online; many Kashmiris abroad look for doctors for their parents back home. Doctors, business leaders and policymakers read Bazar Observer. And students and young families follow Young Kashmir, Mizraab and our social media. No other single platform covers all four.

## Slide 7: Who we should approach · 4:20 – 5:00

**I suggest we approach five types of advertisers.** Private hospitals want to promote new departments and specialists. Diagnostic labs want walk-ins for health packages. Pharma and wellness brands want seasonal recall. Insurers want to explain cashless claims. And specialty clinics, like dental, skin, IVF and eye, want local patients. I'd start with hospitals and labs because they decide fastest and spend the most.

## Slide 8: Why Kashmir Observer · 5:00 – 5:45

**Why should they choose us?** Because a health decision is a trust decision, and trust is what Kashmir Observer has built over more than twenty-seven years. We are not asking them to take a risk. A health brand has already done it: Paras Health ran its "Mirchi Bachpan Bachao" campaign on our front page on 7 October 2025. That page on the screen is our best case study. With your permission, I'd like to turn it into a one-page success story for the sales team.

## Slide 9: The packages · 5:45 – 6:40

**To make the sale easy, I've built three ready packages, all priced from our 2026 rate card.** The Clinic Starter at sixty-eight thousand rupees gives a small clinic four local print ads and four social posts. The Hospital Growth package, at two lakh, adds a website banner and Observer TV videos. That is the one I expect most hospitals to buy. The Flagship Health Partner, at about seven-point-eight lakh, includes front-page ads, a three-month top banner and a video series. All prices are before GST, and any discount would need your approval.

## Slide 10: Beyond ads · 6:40 – 7:15

**Health brands don't just want ads; they want to educate people.** So I propose four formats: a weekly "Health Matters" page with doctor columns; an "Ask the Doctor" series on Observer TV, which our channel can produce from twenty-five thousand per video; campaigns around World Cancer Day, World Health Day, World Heart Day and World Diabetes Day; and campus health drives through Young Kashmir. One rule protects our credibility: all sponsored content is clearly labelled.

## Slide 11: 90-day plan · 7:15 – 7:55

**Here is how we start.** In the first month we shortlist forty prospects and prepare a one-pager and the Paras Health case study. In the second month we meet fifteen decision-makers and offer a one-month pilot tied to an awareness day. In the third month we deliver the pilots, share a reach report, and convert the best into quarterly or annual partners. The target: forty prospects, fifteen meetings, five pilots, two annual partners. These are planning numbers you can adjust to our real conversion rates.

## Slide 12: What it could be worth · 7:55 – 8:25

**What could this be worth?** If we sign ten Starter clients, five Growth clients and one Flagship partner, that is about twenty-four point six lakh rupees at card rates. This is a scenario to show the size of the opportunity, not a forecast. The Starter package brings volume; Growth and Flagship bring value.

## Slide 13: Risks · 8:25 – 8:55

**I also want to be honest about the risks.** Some private hospitals face delays in SEHAT payments, so we should ask for advance or staged payment and spread across labs, pharma and insurers. Health ads face strict rules, so we need a simple checklist with no cure claims. We must keep sales separate from the newsroom. And to prove results, every ad should carry a QR code or helpline number.

## Slide 14: Our ask · 8:55 – 9:30

**So my request is simple.** One: approve healthcare as our first sector vertical. Two: approve the three packages and a discount band for pilots. Three: let us set up five pilot meetings in the next thirty days, starting with one hospital, one lab and one pharma distributor. As our own rate card says, we don't just deliver news; we deliver trust, credibility and results. Thank you. I'd be glad to take your questions.

**Do:** stop talking, smile, wait for questions.

## Likely questions from the Editor, with short answers

1. **"Why not education? It already brings us money."**
   Education is valuable but already saturated; coaching ads are on our front page. Healthcare is new money, not a reshuffle of existing advertisers.

2. **"Won't sponsored health content hurt our credibility?"**
   Only if it isn't labelled. Every partner piece carries a clear "Partner content" tag, the newsroom stays independent, and we reject cure claims. Credibility is the product we are selling, so we protect it first.

3. **"Where did your market numbers come from?"**
   Published reports: Daily Excelsior for the budget, the J&K Economic Survey via Kashmir Life for beds and colleges, Greater Kashmir for SEHAT. The last slide lists them. I'd verify the latest figures before using them with a client.

4. **"Hospitals have payment problems. Will they pay us?"**
   That's why I suggest advance or staged payment, and why the plan includes labs, pharma and insurers, not just hospitals.

5. **"Is ₹24 lakh realistic?"**
   It's a sizing scenario at card rates with sixteen clients, not a promise. Even half of it would justify a dedicated health vertical.

6. **"Which figure is right for the website: 14 million a year or 2 million a month?"**
   *(Check with the team before presenting.)* The media deck says 14 million page views for Oct 2024 – Oct 2025; the 2026 rate card says 2 million a month on average. I used the newer rate card figure.

## Delivery tips

- **Pace:** about 140 words a minute. If you're running long, shorten slides 6, 7 and 10; never skip 8, 9 or 14.
- **Numbers:** say them slowly and round them ("about twenty-four lakh"). Exact figures are on the slides.
- **Slide 8:** point to the Paras Health ad on the front-page image. It's your strongest moment.
- **Rehearse twice with a timer.** Aim for 9 minutes so you have room to breathe.
